# Underdeep — Economies, trade and autonomous settlements (Stage 6)

Spoiler-free. Code: `civ/econ/*` (simulation and persistence), with hooks into `civ/npc` (minds, places,
dialogue, entities). Data: `src/main/resources/data/underdeep/economy/{goods,production}.json`, generated by
`tools/economy/generate_economy.py` (edit the script, re-run, commit both).

## 1. What runs where

| Tier | Which settlements | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Active | players nearby (embodied inhabitants) | the same economic steps as below, plus visible work, physical trades with players, physical caravans on routes near players, block-by-block construction in loaded chunks |
| Regional | within 512 blocks of a player | hourly economy steps (`economyStepTicks` 1000) |
| Distant | everything else within `economyRadius` (1600) of a player or spawn | coarse steps (`economyDistantStepTicks` 4000); shipments travel abstractly |

Every settlement has **one** `Economy` (in `data/underdeep_economy.dat`, together with shipments, contracts,
property and businesses, so cargo and ledgers are always saved together). No tier keeps its own copy. Steps are
round-robin with a per-tick budget (`economySettlementsPerTick`); catch-up after a pause is capped
(`economyMaxCatchUpHours`, 48) and processed in ≤ 6 h chunks; daily processes run on day boundaries (at most two
per update). Nothing loads chunks.

## 2. Goods, resource categories and currency

156 goods, all real items (vanilla and Underdeep), validated at load (unknown ids are skipped). 22 categories:
grain, produce, meat, fish, prepared food, timber, lumber, stone, building materials, ore, metal, fuel, fibre,
animal products, herbs, medicine, gems, decorative, regional specialty, tools, equipment, luxury.
Nutrition comes from each item's food component. **Currency: the emerald** (Stage 4). Ledgers hold item units
(fractional in the abstract); player-facing trades are always whole items and whole emeralds.

## 3. Ledgers and ownership (physical vs abstract)

Each settlement ledger tracks per good: quantity, reserved, produced/consumed/imported/exported rates (smoothed
per day), desired level and price. Ownership at any moment is explicit:

- **Settlement stores** (the ledger): communal production and imports. Abstract only — there are no physical
  chests that mirror it, so nothing can be withdrawn from a chest *and* remain in the ledger.
- **Merchant shelves**: goods a seller bought wholesale from the stores (till → treasury); what players can buy.
- **Project site stock**: materials moved from the ledger to a construction project (owned by the project).
- **Shipment cargo**: leaves the origin ledger at departure; belongs to the shipment until delivered once.
- **Household storage**: belongings of people who died (never deleted).
- **Player business stock / companion pack**: the inventory *is* the record's list (no copy, no duplication).

Physical world loot (Stage 4 chests in buildings) is separate from the economy and is not counted.

## 4. Production (data-driven, conserving)

102 recipes (27 gathering, 75 processing) over 28 producing professions (plus merchants and guards). A recipe
defines professions, workplace kinds, inputs (a good, a category such as `@LUMBER`, or solid fuel), outputs,
by-products, hours per batch, a per-worker daily cap, skill, region/habitat requirements, a sustainability pool,
tool wear and fuel. Workers produce **only while their mind is actually working** (Stage 5 activity) — so schedules,
health, rest, food and skill all matter. Each worker picks the recipe with the best value × demand whose inputs
exist; missing inputs mark the workshop idle with the reason (and trigger imports). Inputs are consumed exactly as
defined; outputs are bounded by storage and by "do not overproduce" (stock far above the desired level).

**Gathering** brings renewable goods in: outputs such as `@SPECIES` use the actual yields of the region's native
species (Stage 2 ecology: trees, herbs, flowers, shrubs, minerals, crystals; exceptional species never), and
`@CIVCROP` the culture's staple crop. Gathering draws on per-settlement pools (crops, forage, fungi, herbs, wood,
fish, game, ranch, minerals, ore, stone) whose capacity comes from the region's ecology and the settlement's
farms/pens/docks; yields fall as a pool is depleted and recover logistically. **Hunting and fishing also remove
actual animals** from the region's Stage 3 abstract wildlife population, never below 40 % of a species' capacity
(below that, only the pool depletes). Physical crop harvesting by embodied farmers (Stage 4) is cosmetic: the
economy's output is the recipe (no double counting). NPCs never mine or fell trees physically.

## 5. Households, wages and money

- Households buy food every step (≈ 14 nutrition per adult per day, 9 young, 11 elder): prepared food first,
  cheapest per point, from the stores and then from sellers' shelves. Food security (smoothed) is written into
  each member's mind and scales how much meals restore hunger (Stage 5). Hunger weakens (health floor 0.5) but
  never kills. Also fuel (more in the frozen region, none in the volcanic one), a little fibre, medicine for the
  injured (heals), occasional luxuries when prosperous.
- Wages: per productive hour, scaled by skill and prosperity, paid from the treasury (community economy). If a
  household cannot afford food, the treasury covers it (support). Savings above 25 per member pay a 5 %/day levy.
- **Monetary model**: initial reserves (`moneyPerCapita` 30 per inhabitant, split treasury/households/merchants);
  explicit **issuance** when a settlement's money falls below 60 % of target and **upkeep sink** above 160 %
  (5 %/day of target at most); celebrations are a sink; players bring money in by buying and take it out by
  selling. The world audit `total = Σ(initial + issued − sunk + player in − player out)` is checked by the
  self-test (trade between settlements cancels out).

## 6. Prices and merchants

Bounded dynamic prices (`Pricing`): base value × scarcity (free vs desired, square-root response, 0.55–2.2) ×
regional factor (local −10 %, imported +8 % + transport by distance) × prosperity; bounded 0.35–3× base (food
0.5–2×), approached 20 % per hour so single trades never jump prices. Sellers buy at the price and sell at
`ask = price × (1 + margin) × (1 − relationship discount ≤ 12 %)`; they buy from players at a bid that is at most
75 % of the ask, and bundles round in the merchant's favour → **no profitable instant loops** (unit-tested at all
prices). Offers are rebuilt from the real shelf each time a trade screen opens; uses are limited by shelf stock and
the till; each completed trade is mirrored on the server exactly once. Sellers buy only what the settlement is
short of (never tools/equipment, which could be damaged). Sellers sell their trade's categories; general
merchants sell broadly. Shelves keep households' share (reserve) and restock as goods exist.

## 7. Trade network, shipments, caravans

- **Network**: each settlement links to its nearest partners on the same cavern layer within 1800 blocks (3 links,
  5 for towns/cities, which also link hubs up to 3500 blocks). Routes follow the Stage 1 cavern graph (A*), with
  danger from the regions crossed; water connections where both ends are waterside.
- **Decisions**: imports when food falls below 3 days, workshops lack inputs, projects lack materials, medicine or
  fuel run low; exports of real surpluses to partners that are short. The supplier keeps 1.5× its desired food
  and 1.3× other goods; no re-import of goods exported recently and vice versa; one order per good per day.
  Relations, route danger and reliability choose the supplier; strategic goods are not traded during disputes.
- **Shipments** (`Shipment`): cargo leaves the origin at departure; the buyer's payment is escrowed in the
  shipment; on arrival cargo enters the destination once, the seller and the carrier household are paid, the
  remainder refunded; repeated deliveries become **agreements** (every 2 days, 4 deliveries, failures lower
  reliability). Methods: porter (≤ 24 units), pack train (≤ 96), boat (≤ 160; water routes, **abstract only**).
- **Disruptions** (by route danger): delays, wildlife attacks (guards defend; otherwise part of the cargo is lost
  and the trader hurt), blocked passages (the caravan turns back, cargo returns home, buyer refunded). Most trips
  arrive.
- **Physical caravans**: a moving land shipment 40–96 blocks from a player is embodied at its route position:
  the actual trader from the origin (or a hired hand), guards and pack animals (capbacks, leashed; cannot be
  tamed or ridden). They walk the route; their progress is written back to the same shipment; stuck caravans move
  on when unobserved; they vanish into the abstract journey when players are > 128 blocks away. Killing the trader
  loses the shipment (exactly once) and spills half the cargo. Caravan entities are never saved.
- Traders carry **news**: up to three recent facts they know are told to people at the destination.

## 8. Settlements: leadership, decisions, construction, population

- **Leadership**: culturally styled (council of elders, masters of the forge, harbour master, circle of keepers,
  artisans' guild, chieftain, merchant guild, observatory council), chosen from actual adult inhabitants by the
  respect and trust others hold for them, skill, ambition and cultural roles; re-chosen when they leave or die.
- **Decisions** (18 categories, daily, utility with reasons, at most three acted on): import food, increase food
  production, build housing, expand storage, build a workshop, open positions, establish trade, export surplus,
  improve defences, repair infrastructure, expand the waterfront, fund a gathering, support households, post a
  contract, build an inn, expand the market, build a clinic, civic improvement.
- **Construction** (20 project kinds: house, large/small/shared house, workshop, farm, pen, barn, storehouse,
  stall, shop, inn, kitchen, guard post, dock, hall, library, clinic, well, tower, repair). Stage 4 blueprints of
  the settlement's civilization; plot search around the settlement avoiding plan buildings and roads, plazas,
  other projects, player property (+4 blocks) and terrain unsuitable for the building; when the plot is loaded,
  any non-natural block (anything player-made) rejects it — and if something is built there later the project
  stops and refunds. Lifecycle PLANNED → SITE → FUNDED (labour budget) → MATERIALS (goods to site stock; contracts
  for missing ones) → BUILDING (builders' hours: carpenters, stonemasons, engineers and people without a
  workplace choose the new CONSTRUCT activity) → COMPLETE. Blocks are placed bottom-up only while the chunks are
  loaded (`constructionBlocksPerTick`, 16), with the Stage 4 placer's final pass for foundations; buildings
  completed far away are placed when their chunks next load. Completed buildings register beds and job stations;
  repairs fill air at missing blocks of public buildings only.
- **Population**: job openings are filled by the unemployed, then by migrants from partner settlements (the same
  person moves: name, appearance, mind, memories, skills; old relationships kept as "elsewhere" bonds; friends are
  told), then rarely by newcomers from beyond the simulated region. People emigrate for real reasons (no work,
  hunger, danger, wanderlust) to partners with a bed. Close singles may become partners and move in together;
  partners with food and a free bed may welcome a child (rare). Residents of crowded homes move into new houses.
  Grown settlements are recognised as the next category. Deaths free workplaces; belongings stay with the
  household. No ageing.

## 9. Diplomacy, reputation, events

- Civilization relations (28 pairs; trust, familiarity, cooperation, tension, competition, reliability,
  dependence, history; deterministic historical baseline): deliveries build trust and dependence; failures lower
  reliability; exporting the same categories breeds competition and tension; tension > 60 restricts strategic
  trade for three days; reconciliation when it falls. Local connection trust/reliability as well.
- Player reputation is never a global variable: a settlement's view of a player is computed from its inhabitants'
  own bonds (Stage 5), and spreads only through their conversations and travelling traders.
- **Events** (~40 types, plus per-pool, per-workshop and per-good variations), all from actual state: food
  shortage/recovered, good harvest, depleted pools, idle workshop, price spike, hard times, new deposit, caravan
  departed/arrived/delayed/attacked/lost, route blocked, emergency aid, trade agreement/ended, trade restriction,
  reconciliation, construction planned/started, building completed, repairs planned/done, migrant arrived,
  resident left, new household, partnership, new family member, new apprentice, job change, new merchant, leader
  chosen, settlement grew, celebration, contract posted/completed, property for sale, business opened. Each event
  is known first by the leaders and a couple of others and spreads through Stage 5 conversations; inhabitants talk
  about it in dialogue only because they know it.
- Celebrations (culture-specific) shift schedules (CELEBRATE activity at the square), use food and luxuries,
  and lift moods.

## 10. Players

- **Trade**: buy where goods are abundant, sell where they are scarce (sellers buy what their town lacks).
  Prices seen are noted in the journal with the day (history, never live). "What do you have in stock?" lists
  availability (plenty/normal/scarce, local/imported) and why something is dear.
- **Noticeboard** (leaders and innkeepers; "What's on the noticeboard?"): open contracts, planned construction,
  gatherings, property for sale, openings, warnings; accept contracts, buy/rent property, register a shop,
  "What does the town need?".
- **Contracts** (rewards escrowed from the issuing treasury when posted): supply a shortage, supply construction
  materials, escort a caravan on a dangerous route (paid on arrival if you travelled with it). Hand-ins are
  validated against your inventory and pay once. Up to `maxContractsPerPlayer` (3). Courier contracts exist in the
  data model but are not posted yet.
- **Property**: only buildings without residents or keepers (including newly built ones) are listed; buy or rent
  (7 days, a fifth of the price); money goes to the treasury. `/property [list|share <player>|unshare <player>|
  abandon]`. Containers, doors, beds and blocks inside property are protected from non-members.
- **Shops**: register an owned property as a shop; `/business [status|stock|collect|price cheap|fair|premium]`.
  The stock inventory is the record. NPC households buy what they need with their own money when your price is
  fair. Hire a shopkeeper ("Would you keep my shop?"): they sell your stock to players and draw more customers;
  paid daily from the till; they quit when unpaid twice.

## 11. Configuration (`config/underdeep.json`, Stage 6 section)

`economyEnabled`, `economyRadius`, `economyStepTicks`, `economyDistantStepTicks`, `economyMaxCatchUpHours`,
`economySettlementsPerTick`, `productionRate`, `consumptionRate`, `wageRate`, `priceVariation`,
`merchantMargin`, `tradeFrequency`, `transportCost`, `constructionSpeed`, `constructionBudget`,
`populationGrowth`, `migrationFrequency`, `eventFrequency`, `moneyPerCapita`, `maxActiveCaravans`,
`maxShipments`, `maxProjectsPerSettlement`, `constructionBlocksPerTick`, `maxBusinessesPerPlayer`,
`maxEmployeesPerBusiness`, `maxContractsPerPlayer`, `propertyProtection`. All take effect on the next server start.
`tradeFrequency` is reserved (trade decisions are need-driven).

## 12. Diagnostics (`/underdeep econ …`, operators)

`econ` (summary: prosperity components, food days, beds, vacancies, partners), `ledger`/`prices`, `workforce`
(workplaces, recipes, missing inputs, vacancies, pools), `households`, `merchants`, `decisions`, `events`,
`projects`, `shipments`, `network`, `relations`, `properties`, `businesses`, `contracts`, `audit` (money flows),
`world` (totals, step cost, world log), `advance <hours>`, `build <kind>`, `shortage` (test disruption),
`daily` (run today's decisions now).
