# PRIVATE — Civilizations (SPOILERS)

Full identities of the eight Stage 4 civilizations. Public docs list only names, home ecosystems, building
styles and trades. Histories, customs, symbols and secrets live here. Stage 5 (dialogue, memory) and
Stage 6 (economies, factions) build on these definitions.

Shared background: the underground was once ruled by an older people. Lore.md calls them the
Vaultwrights, and they sealed the sky. The eight cultures are descendants or refugees who arrived after
the sealing. None of them knows the whole story, and each remembers a different fragment (see LORE.md).

---

## 1. Rootfolk (Rootwild; also Emerald Wetlands, Golden Grove, Fungal fringe)
- **History.** Descended from the garden-keepers of the old vaults. They believe the great roots that
  pierce the cavern ceilings are the "ribs of the sky" and still carry light down from above. Their oldest
  village is said to stand where the first root broke through (H-01 Heartwood).
- **Architecture.** Duskoak timber frames on mossy stone plinths, mud-brick lower courses, steep spruce
  roofs thatched with living leaves, lanterns on chains, homes built around living trunks; the Elder Tree
  landmark (spiral stair, ring platform).
- **Palette.** Moss green, bark brown, warm lantern gold.
- **Clothing.** Green tunics, brown trousers, leaf-patterned belts; herbalists wear wide hats.
- **Symbols.** A root curled into a spiral; three leaves.
- **Occupations.** Foresters, carpenters, herbalists, brewers, tailors, farmers.
- **Agriculture.** Duskroot and glowgrain in raised beds; duskberry hedges; they never fell a living elder
  tree.
- **Resources and trade.** Wood sets, herbs, remedies, berries, brewed drinks.
- **Transport.** On foot along root bridges; pack capbacks bought from Sporefolk neighbours.
- **Customs.**
  - Name-days are held under the Elder Tree.
  - Newcomers are offered duskberries.
  - Disputes are settled by planting.
- **Foods.** Baked duskroot, berry pies, cavern stew.
- **Wildlife.**
  - Protective. They tame rootgliders and snufflets.
  - Hunting is allowed only for food, and is never done near nests.
- **Outsiders.** Welcoming and curious; they gossip about travellers.
- **Organization.** Councils of household elders meet in the hall; there is no single ruler.
- **Dialogue themes.** Seasons without a sun, the "sky ribs", gentle teasing about Forgekin smoke.
- **Secret.** The Elder Tree's crown hides a seed vault (Stage 9 hook).

## 2. Forgekin (Ashen Hollows; also Crimson Basin, Stormstone, Petrified)
- **History.**
  - Miners who stayed below when the vaults were sealed, and who were bound by an oath to "keep the
    fires lit".
  - They believe the world will freeze if the great furnaces go out.
- **Architecture.**
  - Blackstone halls on heavy plinths, basalt pilasters, copper cornices, crenellated flat roofs,
    smoking brick chimneys, iron-barred windows.
  - The Great Furnace landmark: a sealed molten core behind tinted glass.
- **Palette.** Soot black, ember orange, copper.
- **Clothing.** Leather aprons, heavy boots, braided beards; guards wear bronze-tinted plate.
- **Symbols.** An anvil inside a flame; a chain of seven links (the oath).
- **Occupations.** Miners, smelters, blacksmiths, engineers, stonemasons.
- **Agriculture.** Emberpeppers near vents; little else. They import food.
- **Resources and trade.** Tools, armour, ingots, emberite, rails and minecarts.
- **Transport.** Minecarts and heavy bridges.
- **Customs.**
  - The oath is renewed at the Great Furnace each year.
  - A broken tool is buried, not discarded.
- **Foods.** Spiced game meat, roasted truffles (bought), emberpepper stew.
- **Wildlife.** Pragmatic. They respect scorchjaws, and fear the slag titan as a "furnace spirit"
  (private: WILDLIFE.md).
- **Outsiders.** Gruff but fair; they trade honestly and distrust flattery.
- **Organization.** Guild masters, plus a Forge-Warden who keeps the oath.
- **Dialogue themes.** Heat, metal, the oath, "the cold that waits".
- **Secret.** The furnaces vent into something below the deep layer (Stage 9/10).

## 3. Tide Dwellers (Flooded Depths, Sunken Gardens; also Emerald Wetlands)
- **History.**
  - Boat-folk who escaped a flood of the lower vaults.
  - Their navigators keep star-charts of the cavern ceilings, the glowing ceiling patterns they call
    "the drowned sky".
- **Architecture.**
  - Birch and mirewillow houses on decks raised by log posts, dark-prismarine hip roofs, sea-lantern
    beacons, piers and moored boats.
  - The Lighthouse landmark.
- **Palette.** Sea green, white, deep blue.
- **Clothing.** Striped shirts, oilskin coats and wide rain hats.
- **Symbols.** A pearl in a cupped hand; a three-pointed oar.
- **Occupations.** Fishers, boatwrights, navigators, tailors, brewers.
- **Agriculture.** Mire rice beside canals, lake chestnuts, sea grapes.
- **Resources and trade.** Fish, pearls, boats, maps, nets (string).
- **Transport.** Boats; lakehorn swimming mounts.
- **Customs.**
  - A lantern is lit for those lost at sea.
  - Pearls are given at weddings.
- **Foods.** Fish fillets, rice bowls, chestnut bread.
- **Wildlife.**
  - They revere the grand drifter as a sign of safe passage (private: WILDLIFE.md).
  - They never harpoon veilrays.
- **Outsiders.** Hospitable traders who love news from far shores.
- **Organization.** Harbour-masters and ship families.
- **Dialogue themes.** Currents, weather in the caverns, the drowned archive (H-03).
- **Secret.** Their oldest chart shows a drained lake with a building beneath it (H-03).

## 4. Sporefolk (Fungal Expanse; also Wetlands, Rootwild)
- **History.** They believe they were "grown" by the mycelium itself. Elders speak with the network
  through meditation.
- **Architecture.**
  - Round mushroom-stem houses with domed caps, glowing spots and gill rims; colourful glowing paths.
  - The Great Cap landmark: a gathering hall under a giant cap.
- **Palette.** Violet, cap teal, spore gold.
- **Clothing.** Purple robes; personal cap-hats, coloured by family.
- **Symbols.** A ring of seven caps; an open gill.
- **Occupations.** Mycologists, alchemists, herbalists, healers, brewers.
- **Agriculture.** Cave truffles in darkness, puffcap beds, spore gardens.
- **Resources and trade.** Remedies, fungi, truffles, brewed tinctures.
- **Transport.** On foot; capback pack animals.
- **Customs.**
  - Guests are dusted with harmless spores as a welcome.
  - The dead are returned to the mycelium.
- **Foods.** Roasted inkcaps, truffle stew, fermented drinks.
- **Wildlife.** Pufflings are kin. They never kill a capback.
- **Outsiders.** Strange but warm, and endlessly curious about "single-bodied" people.
- **Organization.** Circles of elders who decide by consensus.
- **Dialogue themes.** The network's dreams, "the deep root that listens", medicine.
- **Secret.** The network remembers the sealing (Stage 9 dream sequence).

## 5. Glimmerkin (Crystal Deep; also Stormstone, Starlight)
- **History.** Gemcutters who believe crystals store memories. Their libraries hold "singing stones"
  (H-02).
- **Architecture.**
  - Calcite walls with deepslate ribs, tall pointed purpur roofs, amethyst spires with crystal tips,
    luminite light.
  - The Crystal Spire landmark with an observation gallery.
- **Palette.** Pale stone, blue, violet, crystal white.
- **Clothing.** Blue tunics with silver trim and sparkling thread.
- **Symbols.** A six-faceted gem; a tuning fork.
- **Occupations.** Gemcutters, artisans, lampwrights, scholars, smelters.
- **Agriculture.** Glowgrain under luminite light; very little else.
- **Resources and trade.** Opal, glimmer shards, amethyst, jasper, crafted lights, books.
- **Transport.** Bridges over crystal ravines, on foot.
- **Customs.**
  - Each child is given a "first facet" stone.
  - Silence is kept in the libraries.
- **Foods.** Glowgrain bread, mineral teas.
- **Wildlife.** They study prismhorns and protect geode crab colonies.
- **Outsiders.** Polite and reserved; they value precision and honesty.
- **Organization.** A college of masters.
- **Dialogue themes.** Light, memory, harmony; the singing hall (H-02).
- **Secret.** The singing stones answer in chords: order matters (H-02).

## 6. Stoneborn (Hollow Peaks; also Everfrost, Petrified Forest)
- **History.** Mountain clans who carved the first stairways between the cavern layers. They say every
  stair they build "keeps the world together".
- **Architecture.**
  - Heavy stone-brick plinths, cobbled-deepslate walls with quoins, deep-eaved deepslate-tile roofs,
    shuttered windows, terraced placement into slopes.
  - The Hall of Steps landmark.
- **Palette.** Grey stone, rust red, frost white.
- **Clothing.** Red and grey fur-trimmed coats; braided beards.
- **Symbols.** A stair of three steps; crossed picks.
- **Occupations.** Stonemasons, blacksmiths, hunters, miners, tailors.
- **Agriculture.** Frost turnips, cliffberries, snowberry hedges.
- **Resources and trade.** Building stone, durable tools, hides, furs.
- **Transport.** Long stairways, cliffstrider climbing mounts, pack animals.
- **Customs.**
  - A stone is added to the family cairn at every birth.
  - Debts are carved into the hall stair.
- **Foods.** Baked turnips, game meat, snowberry jam.
- **Wildlife.** Hunters who follow strict seasons; ledgewings are honoured messengers.
- **Outsiders.** Wary at first; once trust is earned, extremely loyal.
- **Organization.** Clan chiefs and a stair-keeper.
- **Dialogue themes.** Endurance, clans, the "first stair".
- **Secret.** The first stair leads to the sky-seal (H-06, Stage 10).

## 7. Amberfolk (Golden Grove; also Rootwild clearings, Wetlands)
- **History.** Merchant-farmers who kept the old roads and markets alive after the sealing. Their
  guild-ledgers are the oldest continuous records underground.
- **Architecture.**
  - Goldwood half-timbering with white infill, brick roofs, cut-sandstone ground floors, flower boxes,
    awnings.
  - The Fountain Court landmark with an arcade.
- **Palette.** Gold, warm white, brick red.
- **Clothing.** White shirts, amber vests and sashes; farmers wear straw hats.
- **Symbols.** A balanced scale over a sheaf; an amber drop.
- **Occupations.** Farmers, tailors, carpenters, brewers, artisans, bakers, merchants.
- **Agriculture.** Sunbeans, glowgrain, honeyfruit orchards, goldbough resin.
- **Resources and trade.** Food, textiles, regional commerce, amber.
- **Transport.** Caravans (Stage 6), pack animals and glade runners.
- **Customs.**
  - Market days.
  - Harvest feasts open to all.
  - Contracts are sealed with amber.
- **Foods.** Bread, stews, honeyfruit tarts, sunbean stew.
- **Wildlife.** They herd goldfleece and keep bees (bumblewisps) in orchards.
- **Outsiders.** The most welcoming; every traveller is a customer.
- **Organization.** A merchant council, plus guilds.
- **Dialogue themes.** Prices, harvests, gossip from the roads.
- **Secret.** The first ledger names the Vaultwrights as customers (LORE hook).

## 8. Astrals (Starlight Caverns; also Crystal Deep)
- **History.**
  - Scholars of the "false stars", the glowing ceilings.
  - They believe the starlight is a message left by those who sealed the sky, and they are trying to
    read it.
  - They are the culture closest to the truth, and the most secretive.
- **Architecture.**
  - Calcite and quartz with starsilver trim, colonnades, tall arched windows, light-blue domes topped
    with end rods, glowing gardens.
  - The Observatory landmark.
- **Palette.** Pale stone, blue, silver.
- **Clothing.** Hooded light-blue and white robes embroidered with star points.
- **Symbols.** An eye over seven dots (their reading of a ceiling constellation).
- **Occupations.** Astronomers, scholars, lampwrights, cartographers, artisans.
- **Agriculture.** Glowgrain and sunbeans under starstone light; moonpetal gardens.
- **Resources and trade.** Maps, books, starsilver, lights, spyglasses.
- **Transport.** Delicate bridges; on foot.
- **Customs.**
  - Nightly "readings" at the observatory.
  - Names are given after constellations.
- **Foods.** Light meals, moonpetal draughts.
- **Wildlife.** They follow the sky drifter's routes (private: WILDLIFE.md) and protect starwisps.
- **Outsiders.** Courteous and evasive; they share knowledge only with those who have proven patient.
- **Organization.** An order of readers led by the eldest observer.
- **Dialogue themes.** Patterns, prophecy and the sky.
- **Secret.** Their star-charts point to the sky-seal and the Heartwood (H-01, H-06).

## Settlement naming
Each culture has its own syllable set and pattern (`civ/Names`). The settlement nearest spawn is always a
welcoming culture (Rootfolk, Amberfolk, Sporefolk or Tide Dwellers).
